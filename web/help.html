Help

Taking a good photo

  • Photograph one leaf, filling most of the frame.
  • Use even light. Avoid strong shadows, glare and flash reflections.
  • Keep the leaf in focus and include the area with symptoms.
  • A plain background, such as a sheet of paper, helps.

Reading the result

LeafDoctor shows its three most likely classes with a confidence bar each. Confidence is calibrated, but a high number does not mean the answer is right.

If the top confidence is below the app's threshold, you see “Unsure — ask a local agricultural expert”. On real field photos this happens most of the time, by design: in testing on field photos the model was right only about one time in five overall, and about four to five times in ten when it was confident enough to answer.

The “what to look for” line describes typical signs of the top class so you can compare with your leaf. It is not treatment advice.

What it can recognise

27 classes: healthy leaves and common diseases of apple, blueberry, cherry, corn, grape, peach, bell pepper, potato, raspberry, soybean, squash, strawberry and tomato. Any other plant or problem will be forced into one of these classes, so expect wrong answers for anything else.

Privacy and offline use

The model runs in your browser with ONNX Runtime Web. Photos are never uploaded. After the first visit the app and model are stored on your device and work without a connection; you can also add it to your home screen.

Limitations

  • Trained only on PlantVillage lab photos (single leaves on plain backgrounds).
  • Field photos with several leaves, soil, hands or fruit reduce accuracy a lot.
  • Not a substitute for an expert. For decisions about your crop, contact a local agricultural extension service or plant clinic.
